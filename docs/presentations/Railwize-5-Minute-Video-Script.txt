RAILWIZE — FIVE-MINUTE PROJECT PRESENTATION

Read the spoken paragraphs aloud. The timestamps and [screen cues] are for
you, not for narration. Aim for a calm pace of about 125–135 words per minute.
The timing is approximate; leave short pauses while switching screens.

Before recording: keep a passenger account and an admin account ready in
separate browser sessions. Preload a search with results, a nearby/connecting
option, an existing confirmed ticket, and the admin dashboard. Choose a valid
future journey date from your loaded catalogue. Use the prepared ticket to
explain the payment outcome instead of waiting through checkout on camera.


0:00–0:30 | INTRODUCTION
[Show the Railwize home page.]

Hello everyone. Today, I'm presenting my project, Railwize, a railway
reservation platform built using Angular and Spring Boot.

I wanted to solve a practical problem: when a suitable direct train is
unavailable, passengers have to manually check nearby stations and compare
connecting trains. Railwize brings that search together, along with booking,
payment tracking and journey updates, in one application.


0:30–1:00 | SEARCH EXPERIENCE
[Show a prepared train search and its class-wise results.]

Let me start with the passenger journey. Users can register with email
verification or sign in through Google.

To search, I only need the source, destination and travel date. The results
show the available classes, seat availability and per-person fares. After
choosing a class, I enter the passenger count and details. Saved passengers
can be selected from the Master List, which makes repeat bookings faster.


1:00–2:00 | THE DISTINCTIVE FEATURE
[Show nearby-station results, followed by a prepared connecting itinerary.]

The main feature I want to highlight is alternative journey discovery.

For example, someone may search from Mumbai Central to Old Delhi. A train
arriving at nearby New Delhi could still be useful. My search considers
nearby stations at both ends, so it can suggest a different departure
station, arrival station, or both.

It also supports journeys with one interchange. For example, a journey from
Hazrat Nizamuddin to Varanasi can connect through Lucknow Junction. The
system checks the operating dates, arrival and departure times, transfer
window and availability before suggesting it.

The results show station names alongside codes. Each leg has its own booking
and PNR, so passengers can clearly understand what they are booking. This
reduces the effort of comparing separate searches manually.


2:00–3:00 | BOOKING, WAITLIST AND UPDATES
[Show the passenger-entry page, then an existing ticket and notifications.]

Once the passengers are selected, the backend calculates a fresh fare and
creates a payment hold for up to two minutes. I integrated Razorpay in test
mode, so no real money is involved. The backend verifies payment before
activating the booking.

The platform supports confirmed and waitlisted bookings. I keep everyone in
one booking together. Paid waitlisted bookings are automatically confirmed
in queue order when enough seats become available for the whole group.

If a booking is still waitlisted at the cutoff, it is cancelled and refunded
in full.

Passengers can download a PDF ticket, see coach and berth details, and add
their journey to a calendar. Booking confirmation includes an emailed PDF
ticket. Cancellation, refund and waitlist-promotion updates are also
communicated through email and in-app notifications.


3:00–3:40 | ADMINISTRATOR FEATURES
[Switch to the admin dashboard, then show dated train cancellation.]

On the administrator side, I provide a workspace to manage stations,
trains, timetables, users and bookings. The dashboard includes booking,
cancellation and revenue statistics, popular trains and downloadable CSV
reports.

An important operation is cancelling a train for a specific departure date.
Affected passengers are notified and receive full refunds without a
cancellation deduction. Other departure dates remain available.

Admin access is controlled through initial setup and invitations. A user
cannot simply choose the admin role during public registration.


3:40–4:15 | ASSISTANT AND SUPPORT
[Open Ask Railwize, then briefly show the support-ticket section.]

I also integrated a Gemini-powered assistant. It retrieves platform FAQs
for policy questions and uses protected backend tools for information such
as train availability and the logged-in user's bookings. It can remember
recent conversation context, but it does not make payments or cancel
tickets on the user's behalf.

For issues that need human attention, passengers can raise support tickets,
optionally link a booking, and follow the administrator's replies and
resolution status.


4:15–5:00 | TECHNICAL DESIGN AND CLOSING
[Show the architecture slide from the presentation, then return to the home page.]

Technically, the Angular frontend communicates through an API Gateway with
twelve Spring Boot services. MySQL stores business data, Eureka supports
service discovery, and RabbitMQ carries booking events for email delivery.

I use JWT authentication, backend role and ownership checks, database
locking and duplicate-request protection. Testing includes JUnit, Mockito,
integration tests, Jasmine and Playwright.

Railwize manages its own catalogue and inventory; it is not connected to
Indian Railways' live reservation system.

My main contribution is combining practical journey alternatives with a
complete reservation lifecycle, from finding a train to tracking refunds
and getting support. Thank you.
